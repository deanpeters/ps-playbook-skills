# Exploring the Solution Space Prompts

**Source:** Productside Playbook, B2B work-in-progress deck (`wip-b2b-2026-11`), slides 51-55. Not yet ratified as canonical.

These are copy-paste prompts for the three moments around a solution ideation session, for any chat-based AI tool. You don't need this skill installed to use them. If the skill *is* installed, it already behaves this way. It scopes your How Might We questions, plans the session with you, and challenges your prioritized solution afterward. It never brainstorms the ideas for your team.

The Playbook's rule for every AI page: **you do the thinking, and AI pressure-tests it.**

## How AI Supports This Play

This is where solutions are finally fair game. AI helps you arrive ready. It sharpens the How Might We questions you draft so they run wide enough to invite real options and tight enough to give the brainstorm edges. It also preps you to run a room full of engineers who would rather kill ideas than consider them.

The ideas belong to your team in the session, never to a model beforehand. Once the room produces a prioritized set of hypotheses, AI turns critic and challenges each one on value, viability, and feasibility, so your shortlist survives scrutiny, not just your enthusiasm.

What stays with you is every choice that matters: which question to ask, who is in the room, and which hypotheses are worth testing. AI questions and challenges. You and your stakeholders decide.

| Use AI when | Avoid AI when |
|---|---|
| You have a validated problem statement and want to scope your How Might We questions, plan the ideation session, or stress-test the ideas it produced. | You have no validated problem yet, or you are tempted to let AI generate the solutions your team should create together. |

**Inputs you provide**

- Your validated problem statements (Problem Identification and Framing the Problem)
- Your draft How Might We questions, scoped by you first
- The prioritized solution hypotheses your team generated in the session

**Outputs to expect**

- How Might We questions tested for scope, too broad or too narrow
- A session plan built for quantity, safety, and engagement
- Each hypothesis challenged on value, viability, and feasibility

## Common Traps

| AI brainstorms instead of the team | AI ranks the solutions for you |
|---|---|
| Ideas arrive from the model | The model judges value, viability, and feasibility, not you |
| The team never really engages | The shortlist becomes AI's pick |
| Nobody owns what AI produced | Enthusiasm replaces real evidence |
| The room's feasibility knowledge is wasted | The decision is quietly outsourced |
| Generic options, none grounded in context | |
