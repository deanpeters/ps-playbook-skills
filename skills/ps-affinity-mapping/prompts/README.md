# Affinity Mapping Prompts

**Source:** Productside Playbook, B2B work-in-progress deck (`wip-b2b-2026-11`), slides 38-39. Not yet ratified as canonical.

This is a copy-paste prompt for learning and running affinity mapping in any chat-based AI tool. You don't need this skill installed to use it. If the skill *is* installed, it already behaves this way. It coaches you through the method by default, and it groups notes itself only when you ask, labeling that grouping as a draft for your team to challenge.

The Playbook's rule for every AI page: **you do the thinking, and AI pressure-tests it.**

## How AI Supports This Play

Affinity mapping often starts slow: a pile of real notes and no clear way in. AI acts as a live guide through the method, walking you through each grouping decision as you work, one note or cluster at a time.

Its job is to ask the questions that sharpen your reasoning. Why does this note belong here? What separates this group from that one? Is this a real pattern or just a coincidence? Working through those questions out loud is what turns a stack of notes into themes you can defend.

The clustering, the labels, and the insights you draw are still your calls. AI makes the method less intimidating. It does not make the decisions for you.

| Use AI when | Avoid AI when |
|---|---|
| You have real interview or research notes and know grouping needs to happen but don't know how to start. | You don't have real notes yet. Guiding you through a method on fake data teaches nothing worth learning. |

**Inputs you provide**

- Your real interview notes from Empathy Interviews
- Any rough groupings or first instincts you already have, even incomplete
- Your reasoning, out loud, as you decide what belongs where

**Outputs to expect**

- Guidance through the affinity mapping process
- A completed set of grouped themes with labels

## Common Traps

| AI gives you the insights | Guided is mistaken for validated |
|---|---|
| You paste notes and accept AI's groups | Finishing the steps feels like proof |
| AI's themes quietly become yours | Confidence rises, evidence does not |
| Grouping looks authoritative and goes unchecked | No one re-checks the finished map |
| You skip the sense-making | Process rigor replaces data rigor |

## How to Use This Prompt

1. **Bring your real notes.** Have your Empathy Interview notes in front of you, along with any rough first instincts about what groups with what.
2. **Run the prompt.** Let the coach walk you through the method one step at a time. Answer its questions, do the grouping and labeling yourself, and treat its examples as starting points, not answers.
3. **Commit your themes.** Record your grouped, labeled themes in the template ([../template.md](../template.md)), each one you can explain and defend. Then write at least three key insights.

| # | Prompt | Goal | You bring | You commit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [01-learn-affinity-mapping.md](01-learn-affinity-mapping.md) | Learn affinity mapping by doing it: turn real notes into themes you can defend | Real notes, first instincts, your reasoning | Grouped, labeled themes |

The prompt coaches the method through naming themes. Key insights come after, and they're yours to write.

**Recommended tool type:** chat-based AI tool. Run it with your cross-functional team in the room if you can. The Playbook recommends affinity mapping as a team exercise.
